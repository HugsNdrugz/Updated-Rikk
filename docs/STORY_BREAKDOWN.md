# My Nigga Rikk: Game Vision Deep Dive

## Game Title & Its Significance
The streets got a name for guys like Rikk, and for the game he plays every damn day: "My Nigga Rikk." Yeah, it’s raw, it’s in your face. It’s meant to be. This ain’t no fairy tale; this is the pavement, the hustle, the life. It’s a title designed to make some folks uncomfortable, make 'em think twice. That’s the point. It’s a handshake and a warning, all in one.

*   **"My Nigga"**: This ain't just slang; it's a currency out here. It’s the bond between two cats watchin' each other's backs when the world’s tryna chew ‘em up. It’s the casual nod that says, "I see you, you part of this struggle too." But flip it, and it’s a blade – a way to cut someone down, put 'em in their place. That razor's edge? That’s the world Rikk lives in. Trust is gold, but a knife in the back is just another Tuesday. This game, this title, it’s steeped in that reality. *Developer's Note: The language is a deliberate artistic choice, reflecting the specific vernacular and harshness of the game's world. It's about authenticity and immersion, not offense. The discomfort it might provoke is an intentional part of confronting the game's themes.*
*   **"Rikk"**: Just Rikk. No capes, no chosen one bullshit. He’s the dude on the corner, the one you might overlook, trying to make a dollar outta fifteen cents. Short, to the point, like the patience of a fiend lookin' for a fix. It’s a name that blends in, just like Rikk tries to when the `Heat`’s on.

This title ain't just letters on a screen. It’s the game's opening statement: you’re stepping into Rikk’s worn-out kicks, into a world that doesn’t play fair, where every face could be a friend or a foe, and every choice has a price.

## Core Theme: The Grind & The Hustle in a Precarious World
This is the heart of it, the rhythm Rikk lives by: the relentless **Grind** and the sharp-edged **Hustle**, all played out in a world that’s one bad deal away from falling apart on him. It's about the daily fight to stack enough paper to breathe, in a City that’s always trying to suffocate you with its concrete and its indifference. The opportunities are few and far between, usually tucked away in the shadows, and the system? Man, the system feels like it was designed to watch you fail.

*   **Precariousness**: Everythin's on shaky ground. The `Cash` flow today might be a drought tomorrow. The alley that was safe last week? Now it’s crawling with narcs. Security ain’t somethin' Rikk can buy; it’s somethin' he gotta snatch in moments, and it never lasts. This ain't just about physical danger; it's the gnawing uncertainty of it all.
*   **The Hustle**: This ain't just slingin' product. It’s a dance. It’s readin' people – the jittery fiend, the too-smooth high roller, the snitch lookin' to trade a secret for your freedom. It’s knowing when to push, when to fold, when a smile is genuine and when it’s hiding a blade. It’s about being quick on your feet, turning bullshit into opportunity, and sometimes, yeah, bending the rules 'til they scream, because survival doesn’t come with a manual.
*   **The Grind**: Wake up, hustle, sleep (if you’re lucky), repeat. It’s the small wins that keep Rikk going – that extra twenty from a good haggle, the new contact that slides him some quality goods. But it’s also the setbacks – the deal gone sour, the `Heat` creepin' up, the customer who ODs on your product. It’s a slow, exhausting climb up a ladder that’s missing half its rungs.
*   **Moral Ambiguity**: Ain't no saints in this game, Rikk included. He’s gotta do what he’s gotta do. That might mean selling poison to someone already halfway to hell, or lowballing a desperate soul for a quick buck. The player ain’t here to be judged; they’re here to walk in Rikk’s shoes, make those impossible choices, and live with the weight of ‘em. Right and wrong? Those are luxuries for folks in cleaner streets.

## Narrative & Story Unfolding Through Gameplay
#### Purpose & Goal
Forget a story on rails. Rikk's tale ain't written in stone; it's scrawled on the grimy walls of the City with every deal he makes, every risk he takes. The purpose here is to let the player *live* Rikk's story, to feel it emerge from the chaos of his daily grind. The goal is for each playthrough to spin a unique yarn, woven from the threads of player choices and the unpredictable pulse of the streets. This is how the game truly captures the raw, uncertain nature of the hustle – not by telling you a story, but by letting you build one, piece by precarious piece.

Rikk’s life isn't a movie script; it's a series of moments, of faces in the dim light of a phone screen, of risks taken and prices paid. The story of his struggle, his small victories, and his crushing defeats emerges from the gameplay itself:

*   **Customer Interactions**: These ain't just transactions; they're windows into the soul of the City. Every customer, from the desperate `DESPERATE_FIEND` to the calculating `INFORMANT`, carries a piece of the narrative. Their words, their needs, the very items they peddle or crave – they all paint a picture of their lives, their struggles, the quiet desperation or fleeting joys that mark their days.
    *   **Elaboration**: Imagine a `REGULAR_JOE`, usually chill, starts showing up more agitated, asking for stronger stuff, his dialogue hinting at a job loss or a new stressor. Does Rikk feed the need, offer a weaker alternative, or cut him off? These choices don't just affect `Cash`; they nudge that Joe's personal story, and Rikk's connection to it. Perhaps later, that Joe, if treated with a modicum of respect, offers a crucial tip-off that saves Rikk from a bust. Or, if exploited, he becomes a new kind of risk. These aren't grand quests, but the small, human dramas that make up the fabric of the streets.
*   **`World Events`**: The City breathes, and sometimes it coughs up trouble, sometimes opportunity. A "Police Crackdown" isn't just a stat change; it's Rikk feeling the walls close in, his usual spots suddenly too hot, his phone blowing up with warnings from his network (if he's cultivated one). A "New Drug Fad" could mean a gold rush, but also new dangers, new rivals. These events are the headlines of Rikk's life, the external forces that constantly rewrite his plans.
*   **Player Choices & Consequences**: Every tap on Rikk's phone is a choice. Who to deal with? The `SNITCH` offering good money for that burner phone, knowing it'll bring more `Heat`? Or the low-profit, low-risk deal with a `REGULAR_JOE`? Does Rikk help the `DESPERATE_FIEND` who's clearly on their last legs, maybe giving them a break they don't deserve, or does he squeeze every last cent? These decisions don't just echo in Rikk's wallet; they ripple through his reputation (`Street Cred`), the attention he draws (`Heat`), and the way the world and its inhabitants look at him next time.
    *   **Elaboration**: Consistently choosing high-profit, high-risk deals might paint Rikk into a corner where only the most dangerous, high-`Heat` customers will deal with him, leading to a tense, paranoid playstyle. Conversely, focusing on lower-risk, community-building deals (if such options exist via specific customers or objectives) might build a different kind of narrative, perhaps one of quiet survival and earned respect, but with slower financial gains.
*   **Implied Narrative**: The game doesn't need a narrator spelling out the Big Picture. The player *feels* it. The systemic rot, the poverty, the desperation – it's in the quality of goods people are selling, the prices they're willing to pay, the stories they tell in hushed tones. The graffiti on the wall of a (conceptual) loading screen, the distant sirens, the news ticker on Rikk’s phone – it all adds up.

Rikk's story is a raw tapestry woven from these threads. Maybe he's trying to scrape together enough to disappear, to pay off a debt that hangs over him like a storm cloud, or maybe he's just trying to see the sunrise tomorrow. A sticky note on his phone's home screen, a recurring dream sequence between turns, or a rare, introspective comment from Rikk himself might hint at this deeper motivation, giving the player a quiet anchor in the storm of the daily grind.

#### Thematic Integration
This emergent narrative approach is the lifeblood of the game's themes. The precariousness of Rikk's world is mirrored in the unpredictable nature of customer needs and `World Events`. The "illusion of choice" becomes palpable when every option seems to carry a downside. Moral ambiguity isn't a dialogue option; it's the gut feeling after selling to someone you know you're harming, just to make your own ends meet. The story becomes a testament to survival in a system that’s not built for you to win.

## The City as a Living (albeit Abstracted) Entity
#### Purpose & Goal
The City ain't just a backdrop; it's the whole damn stage, and sometimes it feels like it's got its own agenda. The purpose of the abstracted City is to make its presence a constant factor in Rikk's hustle – a source of pressure, opportunity, and an ever-watchful eye. The goal is to make the player feel the urban environment's pulse, its dangers, and its fleeting moments of vibrancy, all without needing to render a single street corner in 3D. It’s about the *feel* of the City, the weight of it.

This ain't a city Rikk strolls through. It’s a web of unseen connections, rumors, and dangers, all funneling through his phone. Its lifeblood is information, its breath the sirens in the distance, its mood swings the `World Events` that can make or break a hustler.
*   **Customer Dialogue**: Rikk’s clients are his eyes and ears. They bring the street to him, their words painting pictures of squad cars lurking where they shouldn't be, or a new batch of product hitting the block that’s got everyone buzzing. "Watch your back on 3rd, Rikk, saw a couple new faces askin' 'bout you."
*   **`World Events`**: These are the City's headlines made real for Rikk. A "Police Crackdown" means the streets are tight, and every deal feels like walking a razor's edge. A "New Drug Fad" and suddenly Rikk's phone is blowing up, but so is the risk.
*   **Item Availability/Demand**: The City's hunger dictates the market. When a "Port Strike" `World Event` hits, those imported trinkets dry up, and suddenly that dusty "Antique" watch Rikk copped for cheap looks a lot more valuable.
*   **`Heat` & `Street Cred` Systems**: These are Rikk’s vital signs within the City’s ecosystem. High `Heat`? The City feels like it's closing in, every shadow a potential threat. High `Street Cred`? Doors open, whispers turn friendly, the City offers a sliver of respect.
*   **Ambient Phone UI**: Rikk’s phone isn't just for calls; it’s a scanner picking up the City's static. News alerts about a bust in another ward, social media murmurs about a new potent strain, even cryptic text messages from unknown numbers – they all build the atmosphere of a city that never truly sleeps, and is always watching.
    *   **Elaboration - Making the City Felt**:
        *   *`Heat` Zones (Future)*: If Rikk burns a spot with too much `Heat`, that "zone" (even if just a tag affecting certain customers or item prices) stays hot. Customers from that area might be scarce, or only offer desperate, low-ball deals. The City remembers.
        *   *Soundscape (Implied)*: While the game is UI-based, the *implication* of sound is powerful. Dialogue lines can hint at it: "(Sounds of sirens approaching) Gotta go, Rikk!" or "Damn, someone's blasting music loud tonight, can barely hear myself think!" These paint a richer sensory picture.
        *   *Gentrification Event Chain*: This isn't just a price change; it's the City's face morphing. Fewer `DESPERATE_FIEND`s, more `PSYCHEDELIC_EXPLORER` yuppie types looking for "authentic" experiences. The old spots get too clean, too policed. Rikk has to adapt his hustle, find new corners, or get priced out like everyone else. The City doesn't care about his grind; it just changes.

The City is the concrete jungle, beautiful and brutal. It offers the ecosystem for Rikk's hustle but also represents the systemic pressures – the gentrification pushing out the old ways, the policing that targets some more than others, the economic tides that lift some boats and drown others.

#### Thematic Integration
The City, as an abstracted entity, is the embodiment of systemic pressure and the precarious environment. Rikk is a product of it, shaped by its neglect and its harsh opportunities. Its abstracted nature reinforces the feeling of being caught in a system too large to fully comprehend or control, where survival depends on reading the subtle signs and reacting, always reacting.

## The Customers ("Fiends" & Archetypes)
#### Purpose & Goal
These ain't just NPCs; they're the life and death of Rikk's hustle. The purpose of the diverse customer archetypes is to populate Rikk's world with a spectrum of humanity, each reflecting different facets of the struggle, the desire, and the desperation that fuels the street economy. The goal is for each interaction to be a unique blend of opportunity and risk, a chance to profit or a moment that tests Rikk's moral compass, making the player feel the human element in every transaction.

They slide into Rikk’s DMs, each one a story, a need, a potential problem. They are the ecosystem Rikk survives in.
*   **Archetypes**: Each template in `customer_templates.js` is a blueprint for a slice of city life:
    *   The `DESPERATE_FIEND`, chasing the dragon with shaking hands and hollow eyes, their dialogue a mix of raw need and paranoid ramblings. They're a quick buck, but the cost can be heavy, both in `Heat` and on the soul.
    *   The `HIGH_ROLLER`, cool and condescending, looking for the purest product, their transactions a glimpse into a world of wealth and vice far removed from Rikk's own, yet intersecting in these fleeting, high-stakes deals.
    *   The `REGULAR_JOE`, just trying to unwind or find a little something to get through the week, their interactions perhaps the most "normal," yet still tinged with the illegality of it all.
    *   The `INFORMANT`, a creature of shadows and whispers, trading secrets for cash, their loyalty as fleeting as the information they sell. Dealing with them is a gamble – their intel could save Rikk, or their loose lips could sink him.
    *   The `SNITCH`, often cloaked in false concern, their questions too pointed, their presence a constant reminder of the eyes always watching. Every deal with them is a roll of the dice against your `Heat` score.
    *   The `STIMULANT_USER`, a whirlwind of manic energy and half-baked schemes, their dialogue a rapid-fire barrage of ideas and paranoia, their needs urgent and often unpredictable.
    *   The `PSYCHEDELIC_EXPLORER`, seeking cosmic truths in chemical shortcuts, their conversations a blend of philosophical musings and disconnected observations, offering a different kind of escape, and perhaps, a different kind of risk.
*   **Needs & Desires**: Their `buyPreference` isn't just code; it's a reflection of their character. The `DESPERATE_FIEND` needs that Opiate *now*. The `HIGH_ROLLER` demands *uncut* quality. The `PSYCHEDELIC_EXPLORER` wants something to "expand their consciousness." These needs drive the economy Rikk operates in.
*   **Dynamic Behavior**: These ain't static portraits. Moods shift based on Rikk's deals, `World Events`, or their own internal clocks. Addiction isn't just a flag; it's a visible descent, reflected in their dialogue, their increased desperation, the items they might offer to sell (a cherished locket for a hit).
*   **Narrative/Immersion Role**:
    *   **Voices of the City**: They are Rikk's primary source of information about the world beyond his phone screen. Their chatter about police patrols, new drug batches, or local happenings makes the City feel alive and reactive. A fiend might mention how "the new blue stuff is bad news," hinting at a dangerous batch in circulation.
    *   **Thematic Mirrors**: Each customer reflects aspects of the game's core themes. The `DESPERATE_FIEND` is the face of addiction and precariousness. The `HIGH_ROLLER` might represent the corrupting influence of wealth or the hypocrisy of societal elites. The `SNITCH` embodies the theme of betrayal and constant risk. Their stories, even told in fragments, build a mosaic of life on the margins.
    *   **Mini-Narratives**: Successfully navigating a `hasMetRikkBefore` customer, remembering their preferences (even if the game mechanics handle it, the dialogue should reflect it), or seeing their state change due to addiction or Rikk's actions, creates small, satisfying (or unsettling) narrative arcs. For instance, helping a `REGULAR_JOE` who mentions being in a tight spot by giving them a fair price might lead to them later offering a valuable piece of information or a rare item out of gratitude. This makes them feel less like ATMs and more like flawed, reactive individuals.

#### Thematic Integration
Customers are the human face of the game's themes. Their struggles, desires, and the way they interact with Rikk embody the precariousness, moral ambiguity, and systemic issues the game aims to explore. Each transaction is not just economic but a social and potentially ethical choice.

## The Goods (Items)
#### Purpose & Goal
The items in Rikk's world are more than just inventory slots; they are the very currency of his hustle, each telling a story of need, origin, or illicit value. The purpose of the item system is to create a tangible economy that Rikk must master, where understanding the worth, risk, and desirability of each good is key to survival. The goal is to make each item a potential opportunity or a liability, its presence in Rikk's stash a constant reminder of the precarious and often morally grey nature of his business.

These ain't just pixels; they're the lifeblood of the streets, each with its own story, its own weight.
*   **Types & Subtypes**: "DRUGS" aren't just "drugs." There's the quick escape of an "OPIATE," the frantic buzz of a "STIMULANT," the cheap, dirty high of "SYNTHETIC_CANNABINOID." "STOLEN GOODS" range from a "Rolex (Knock-off)," whispering tales of aspiration and deception, to a "Car Stereo (Still Warm)," screaming of petty theft. "INFORMATION" is its own kind of currency, a "Burner Phone" a tool of the trade, "Rival Intel" a weapon. "ODDITIES" like "Sentient Dust Bunny Wisdom" or a "Perfectly Normal Rock (Portal Key?)" inject the surreal, reminding Rikk that the streets have their own strange magic and folklore.
*   **Quality & Value**: That "Standard" quality `aspirin` might just be a headache cure, but "Pure" `cocaine`? That’s a `HIGH_ROLLER`’s ticket, and Rikk's too, if he plays his cards right. The `baseValue` is just a starting point; the real value is what someone’s willing to pay, what Rikk can get away with. The `range` on these values means the market is always a little shifty, just like the people in it.
*   **Effects & Thematic Significance**:
    *   **World-Building**: Each item is a piece of the world. A "Crack Pipe" tells a story of deep addiction. A "Bundle of Fake Cash" speaks of scams and desperation. "Blueprint for Squirrel Armor" (sold by a `STIMULANT_USER`) is a hilarious, tragic glimpse into a mind overclocked and off-kilter.
    *   **Customer Insight**: What a customer wants or sells is a snapshot of their life. The `DESPERATE_FIEND` pawning a "Child's Drawing" for a fix is a gut punch. The `HIGH_ROLLER` casually offering a "Diamond Pinky Ring" shows a different kind of desperation, or just careless wealth.
    *   **Moral Compass**: The items Rikk chooses to stock and sell define his hustle. Is he sticking to "softer" things, or is he pushing the hard, life-destroying stuff for bigger profits? Does he deal in "Stolen Medical Supplies"? Each choice subtly colors the player's journey and Rikk's character.
    *   **Item Descriptions as Narrative**: The flavor text for each item is a chance to inject lore, dark humor, or a sharp observation about the world. "Experimental Nootropic: 'Might make you a genius, might make you forget your own name. Results vary.'" "Stolen Copper Wire: 'Heavy, awkward, and surprisingly easy to cut. Don't ask, don't tell.'" These small details make the goods more than just commodities; they're pieces of the City's story.

#### Thematic Integration
Items are the currency of the hustle, but they are also imbued with thematic meaning. They represent the needs, vices, and aspirations of the City's inhabitants. The choice of what to stock and sell can reflect Rikk's own moral compass (or lack thereof) and his willingness to engage in different levels of risk for profit, directly tying into the core themes of moral ambiguity and precarious survival.

## The Unseen Antagonists & Allies
#### Purpose & Goal
The purpose of these unseen forces is to make Rikk's world feel larger and more interconnected than his direct interactions. They represent the systemic powers and hidden networks that influence his daily grind, even if he never meets them face-to-face. The goal is to create a sense of ambient pressure, unpredictable interference, and occasional unexpected aid, reminding the player that Rikk is a small fish in a dangerous pond, affected by currents he can't always control.

Rikk ain't alone out there, even when it feels like it. There are shadows moving just outside the glow of his phone screen, forces that can help or hurt without ever showing their face.
*   **Law Enforcement (The `Heat`)**: They're more than just a rising number. They're the reason a `REGULAR_JOE` suddenly looks over his shoulder, the reason a deal goes south because the spot is "crawling with pigs." Their presence is in the `World Event` "Police Crackdown" that makes everyone jittery, or the "Corrupt Officer Purge" that might, ironically, make things *more* unpredictable for a while. Rikk feels them in the increased prices from wary suppliers, the informants suddenly demanding more for "risk pay."
*   **Rival Dealers/Crews (e.g., "The Z-Block Kings," "The Crimson Syndicate")**: Rikk's not the only one hustling. Their presence is felt when a customer mentions, "I can get this cheaper from a guy over on 5th," or when a `STIMULANT_USER` tries to sell Rikk "intel" on a rival's stash that might be a setup or a golden opportunity. A `World Event` like "Price War Erupts! Z-Block Floods Market with Cheap Stims" means Rikk's margins get squeezed thin. Or an event like "Crimson Syndicate Leader Arrested" might create a power vacuum, a sudden demand Rikk can fill – if he’s quick and daring enough. Their favored goods (e.g., Z-Block and stimulants, Crimson Syndicate and high-quality opiates) can influence market availability.
*   **Suppliers (via Contacts App)**: Rikk needs his stock. A message from "Big Sal": "My connect just went dry, prices on Zips are through the roof" means Rikk's whole operation takes a hit. Building a good rep with a supplier like "Ghost" (who deals in untraceable goods but at a premium) might mean access to "Pure" quality when everyone else is selling cut, giving Rikk an edge. Losing a supplier could be catastrophic.
*   **Community Figures (e.g., "Mama Carter - Neighborhood Watch")**: These are the neighborhood's ghosts – the old lady who sees everything from her window. Their influence is subtle. Maybe an `INFORMANT` mentions, "Mama Carter said she don't want your kind dealing near the school, Rikk. Just a heads up." Or a positive `World Event` like "Community Vigil Reduces Crime" might temporarily lower `Heat` in an area, an indirect boon from these unseen figures. Their approval or disapproval, tracked by hidden variables based on Rikk's deal locations or types, could trigger these localized events.

These unseen players make Rikk’s world breathe. They’re the reason a sure thing turns sour, or a lifeline appears when he’s about to go under. They ensure the hustle is never just a simple A-to-B.

#### Thematic Integration
These unseen forces are the embodiment of systemic pressures and the interconnected web of the underworld. They highlight Rikk's vulnerability and limited agency against larger machinations – the police system, market forces driven by rivals, the reliability of his supply chain. They also offer glimmers of how community and reputation (even among thieves) can act as a form of precarious support or resistance, adding layers to the moral ambiguity and the ever-present risks and rewards of the hustle.

## Deeper Thematic Layers
#### Purpose & Goal
Beyond the immediate gameplay of buying and selling, the game aims to evoke deeper reflections on the player's part. The purpose of these thematic layers is to give the game a lasting resonance, prompting players to think about the "why" behind Rikk's actions and the nature of the world he inhabits. The goal is to subtly weave these complex issues into the fabric of gameplay and narrative, allowing players to encounter and reflect on them organically.

This ain't just a game about slinging product on a phone. It's about what drives a man like Rikk, the world that shapes him, and the lines that get blurred when survival is on the line.
*   **Systemic Issues**: This is the concrete jungle Rikk was born into. It’s in the background hum of every transaction.
    *   **Gameplay Reflection**: The constant, grinding need for `Cash` isn't just a resource; it’s the weight of poverty. The limited, often illegal, avenues for Rikk to earn reflect a lack of legitimate opportunities. The `Addiction System` isn't just a mechanic; it’s a visible symptom of despair and systemic neglect affecting his customers. A `World Event` like "Funding Cut for Rehab Clinics" makes this even more explicit, potentially increasing the number of `DESPERATE_FIEND`s or their desperation.
*   **Human Connection in Desperation**: Even in the hustle, there are moments. They’re not always pretty, but they’re real.
    *   **Gameplay Reflection**: Rikk might get a dialogue choice to give a `DESPERATE_FIEND` a slightly better price, or just a non-judgmental word. It won't change the world, maybe costs him a few bucks, but it’s a flicker of humanity. Conversely, exploiting that same fiend for every last cent, then seeing them disappear from the customer pool, only for an ambient news report to mention an OD – that’s a connection too, a colder, harder one. The `Loyalty` system with customers could mechanically represent these bonds, or betrayals.
*   **The Illusion of Choice**: Rikk makes choices, sure. But how many of them are *real* choices?
    *   **Gameplay Reflection**: A `World Event` like "Rent Hike City-Wide" suddenly makes Rikk need twice the `Cash` just to stay afloat. His "choice" to take on riskier deals or sell harder drugs becomes less a choice and more a necessity. Or when every customer available is high-risk, high-`Heat`, the "choice" to lay low means starvation. The game should often present Rikk with a menu of bad options, each with its own ugly price tag.
*   **Hope vs. Despair**: This is the razor’s edge Rikk walks. Is there a way out, or is this it?
    *   **Gameplay Reflection**: The grind itself can feel like despair, turn after turn, the numbers barely ticking up. But then a `HIGH_ROLLER` appears, a big score, a flash of `Street Cred` – a moment of hope. A difficult "Win Condition" (like saving a huge sum to "leave the City") offers a long-term, perhaps almost unattainable, hope. Conversely, a string of bad luck, a bust, or seeing a regular customer spiral into oblivion can hammer home the despair. The narrative needs to balance these, ensuring neither entirely eclipses the other.
*   **Gentrification & Change**: The City doesn't stand still. The corners Rikk knew might not be there tomorrow.
    *   **Gameplay Reflection**: A series of `World Events` could signal this: "Old Warehouse District Rezoned for Luxury Lofts," "Increased 'Quality of Life' Policing in Downtown." This could mean Rikk's `REGULAR_JOE` customers get priced out, replaced by new archetypes like "Art Student seeking 'authentic' experiences" or "Tech Bro looking for premium party favors." Old item demands vanish, new ones emerge. Familiar territory becomes hostile, forcing Rikk to adapt or be erased by the changing cityscape. This is systemic change made personal.

#### Thematic Integration
These layers are not separate from the game; they *are* the game. They emerge from the mechanics, the choices, and the stories of the people Rikk meets. The goal is for the player to feel the weight of these themes not as a lecture, but as the lived reality of Rikk's hustle, making each playthrough a unique exploration of survival in a flawed, complex world.

## Core Game Concept & Loop

### Overview
#### Purpose & Goal
The core game loop is designed to simulate the repetitive yet unpredictable nature of Rikk's hustle. Its purpose is to immerse the player in the daily cycle of risk assessment, resource management, and social navigation that defines Rikk's life. The primary goal is to make each turn a series of meaningful choices that directly impact Rikk's survival and standing, reinforcing the theme that every decision matters in a precarious world. The loop should feel like a "grind," but one where player agency can lead to small victories or significant setbacks, mirroring the highs and lows of street-level entrepreneurship.

"My Nigga Rikk" (MNR) is a turn-based, resource management game with strong narrative and RPG elements, played through a minimalist mobile phone interface. The player, Rikk, navigates a precarious urban environment, buying and selling goods, managing relationships with a diverse cast of customer archetypes, and dealing with the pressures of `Heat` (police attention) and maintaining `Street Cred`. The core loop revolves around making strategic choices each turn to maximize profit, manage risk, and progress Rikk's personal objectives (which may be abstract or lightly framed).

### Core Player Objective
#### Purpose & Goal
The player's objectives are designed to reflect the multifaceted nature of survival and ambition in Rikk's world. Profit is essential for immediate survival, but managing risk (`Heat`) and building a reputation (`Street Cred`) are crucial for long-term viability and unlocking new opportunities. Story progression, emerging from these actions, aims to provide a sense of advancement and a deeper understanding of the world and its inhabitants. The ultimate goal is to give the player a sense of agency within a restrictive system, allowing them to define Rikk's success, whether it's material wealth, respect, or simply enduring.

To survive and thrive in a tough urban environment by hustling. This involves:
1.  **Profit Maximization**: Buying low, selling high. This is the fundamental grind, requiring players to learn item values, customer price tolerances, and when to take a less profitable deal to maintain cash flow or build a relationship.
2.  **Risk Management**: Avoiding police attention (`Heat`) and bad deals. This involves choosing customers wisely, sometimes avoiding profitable but high-`Heat` transactions, and potentially investing in ways to reduce `Heat`.
3.  **Reputation Management**: Building `Street Cred` to unlock better opportunities. This is about playing smart, making good on deals, and perhaps cultivating relationships with specific, influential customers.
4.  **Story Progression**: Unlocking new customer types, items, `World Events`, and narrative snippets through actions and achievements.
    *   **Mechanical Elaboration**: Story progression isn't a linear questline but an organic unfolding.
        *   *New Customer Archetypes*: Could be unlocked when `Street Cred` reaches certain thresholds (e.g., `HIGH_ROLLER` only appears if `Street Cred` > 15), or after specific `World Events` occur (e.g., a new drug hits the street, attracting `STIMULANT_USER`s). Some might be unlocked by interacting with an `INFORMANT` who then "introduces" them.
        *   *New Items*: Suppliers (via Contacts App) might offer new product lines if Rikk has proven reliable (good `Street Cred`, successful deals of a certain volume). Some rare items might only appear through specific customer sell offers or `World Events`.
        *   *New `World Events`*: Some events might be gated by game length (Turn number), Rikk's accumulated `Heat` or `Street Cred`, or previous event outcomes, creating an evolving world narrative.
5.  **Potential Long-Term Goal / Win Conditions**: Achieving a specific win-state.
    *   **"The Escape Artist"**: Accumulate $75,000 `Cash`. A new "Offshore Account" Contact appears (unlocked at $50k `Cash` and 20 `Street Cred`). Pay this contact $25,000 for "Relocation Services." Survive 5 more turns with `Heat` below 50. Triggers "Escape" ending cinematic/text, depicting Rikk leaving the life, perhaps with a bittersweet understanding that he can't ever truly leave the memories behind.
    *   **"The Street King/Queen"**: Achieve 100 `Street Cred`. Maintain an average `Heat` below 40 for 10 consecutive turns after reaching 100 `Street Cred`. Have at least $30,000 `Cash`. Triggers "Kingpin" ending cinematic/text, where Rikk is depicted as a respected and untouchable figure in the City's underworld, a legend of the hustle, but still very much part of the life.

The theme and setting are central to the game's message. The purpose is to create an immersive, albeit abstracted, snapshot of a specific socio-economic reality. The mobile phone interface itself is a thematic choice, representing the primary tool of communication and commerce for someone in Rikk's position.

*   **Theme**: The daily grind, the hustle, moral ambiguity, survival in a precarious socio-economic environment. The game aims to be gritty, somewhat dark, but with moments of dark humor or genuine human connection.
*   **Setting**: An abstracted modern urban environment, implied to be somewhat run-down and facing issues like poverty, addiction, and heavy policing. The entire game interface is Rikk's phone.

### Main Game Loop & Turn Progression
#### Purpose & Goal
The turn-based structure serves to break down the "grind" into digestible, decision-focused chunks. Each turn representing a segment of a day reinforces the ongoing, relentless nature of the hustle. The purpose is to create a rhythm where players make key decisions, see immediate consequences, and then face a slightly altered world in the next turn. The goal is to make each turn feel significant, a new chance to profit or a new opportunity for things to go wrong, reflecting the instability of Rikk's life.

The game is turn-based. Each "turn" represents a period of time (e.g., a few hours, half a day).

### Core Turn Sequence
#### Purpose & Goal
The sequence of events within a turn is designed to create a logical flow from macro (world changes) to micro (player actions and their immediate outcomes). The purpose is to ensure that world dynamics can influence player choices for that turn, and that player actions have a clear resolution phase. The goal is to make the game world feel responsive and to structure player decision-making within a consistent framework.

A turn generally follows this sequence:
1.  **Start of Turn / Phone Home Screen**:
    *   Time advances.
    *   Player sees current `Cash`, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, `Turn #`.
    *   Ambient messages or app notifications may appear (e.g., from `data/phone_ambient_ui.js`).
2.  **`World Event` Check**:
    *   The `WorldEventManager` checks if any new `World Events` are triggered or if ongoing events progress/resolve.
    *   Effects of these events (e.g., price changes, item availability, `Heat` changes) are applied to the `GameState`.
    *   Player is notified of significant `World Events` via a phone alert/message.
3.  **Player Action Phase (Interacting with Phone UI)**:
    *   The player interacts with phone apps:
        *   **"Customers" App (Primary Interaction)**:
            *   Triggers `CustomerManager.generateInteraction(GameState)`.
            *   A customer interaction is presented (dialogue, item offer/request).
            *   Player makes choices (buy, sell, haggle, decline).
            *   Outcomes are processed, updating `GameState` (`Cash`, inventory, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, customer `Loyalty`/`mood`, addiction).
            *   This usually consumes the main action for the turn.
        *   **"Stash" App (Inventory Management)**: View items, qualities, potential values. (Does not consume a turn).
        *   **"Contacts" App**: Used to buy supplies, get missions, lower `Heat`, etc. (Interacting with a contact usually consumes the main action for the turn).
        *   **"Map" App**: View district `Heat` levels, event locations. (Does not consume a turn, unless initiating a "travel to meet" action).
        *   **"News" App**: Provides flavor text about City events, `World Events`. (Does not consume a turn).
4.  **End of Turn / Resolution**:
    *   After the primary action (usually a customer interaction or special phone action), the turn effectively ends.
    *   Any turn-based effects are resolved (e.g., addiction cravings increasing, some `World Event` timers ticking down, passive `Heat` decay).
    *   Game state is implicitly saved (or an explicit "End Day" action saves).

### Game Termination
#### Purpose & Goal
Game termination conditions are designed to reflect the harsh consequences of failure in Rikk's world and to provide definitive (though potentially bleak) endpoints to a playthrough. The purpose is not just to end the game, but to reinforce the stakes involved in the hustle. The goal of a "win" condition, if implemented, would be to offer a glimmer of hope or a tangible measure of success against the odds.

*   **Bust**: If `Heat` reaches a critical level, a bust event might be triggered, leading to a game over.
    *   **Mechanical Elaboration**: A "Bust" could result in Rikk losing all (or a significant percentage of) his current inventory and `Cash`. A special "Bust Scene" dialogue or image could be displayed. The game might then present a "score screen" summarizing Rikk's career length (turns survived), peak `Cash`/`Street Cred`, number of deals, etc., before returning to the main menu. There might be different severities of busts, some perhaps allowing a restart with a severe penalty rather than a hard game over.
*   **Financial Ruin**: If `Cash` drops too low for too long (e.g., negative and unable to buy new stock or pay some abstract "living cost" if implemented).
    *   **Mechanical Elaboration**: This could trigger a specific "Game Over - Broke" screen. Similar to a bust, it might show a summary of Rikk's attempts. It emphasizes that the grind is not just about avoiding police, but also about basic economic survival.
*   **Win Condition Met**: Reaching a specific goal.
    *   **Mechanical Elaboration**: As detailed under "Core Player Objective," win conditions like "The Escape Artist" or "The Street King/Queen" provide tangible end-states with narrative weight.
*   **Player Quits**: Standard game exit, ideally saving state.

#### Thematic Integration of Core Loop
The entire core loop, from the turn-based progression to the risk/reward of customer interactions and the looming threat of game termination, is designed to embody the game's central themes. ## Player State & Progression

#### Purpose & Goal
Player state variables (`Cash`, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, Skills) are the core metrics of Rikk's journey. Their purpose is to provide tangible feedback on the player's choices and to gate or influence future opportunities and challenges. The goal is to create a dynamic system where players must juggle competing priorities: making money, avoiding unwanted attention, building a useful reputation, and leveraging their abilities, all of which are essential for navigating the game's precarious world and achieving their objectives.

### Cash
#### Purpose & Goal
`Cash` is the most direct measure of success in the hustle and the primary means of survival. Its purpose is to drive the core economic loop of buying and selling. The goal of the `Cash` mechanic is to constantly pressure the player to make profitable deals while also managing expenses (buying stock, potentially future costs), directly reflecting the financial realities and the "grind" aspect of Rikk's life. Scarcity of `Cash` should lead to more desperate or risky decision-making.

*   **Primary Resource**: Used for all transactions.
*   **Acquisition**: Selling items to customers, successful haggling.
*   **Spending**: Buying items from customers/contacts, potential costs for services (lowering `Heat`, getting info – future).
*   **Game Over Condition**: Dropping to a very low negative value might trigger a game over or severe penalty.
*   **Thematic Link**: `Cash` flow is Rikk's lifeline. Low `Cash` means limited options, inability to capitalize on opportunities, and the constant stress of making rent or affording basic necessities (even if abstracted). It's the tangible reward of a successful hustle and the immediate consequence of failure. The need for `Cash` can push Rikk into making morally grey choices, such as dealing with risky customers or selling potentially harmful goods, perfectly encapsulating the theme of precarious survival.

### Heat
#### Purpose & Goal
The `Heat` system's purpose is to represent the omnipresent risk of law enforcement and the consequences of Rikk's illegal activities. Its goal is to create a dynamic tension, forcing players to balance profitability with caution. It makes the world feel dangerous and Rikk's hustle precarious, as rising `Heat` directly impacts gameplay difficulty and the likelihood of negative outcomes.

*   **Risk Metric**: Represents police attention and general danger.
*   **Increase**: Selling illegal items (especially high-volume or to risky customers like `SNITCH`es), some `World Events` (police crackdowns), getting caught in stings. Specific items (e.g., guns, if implemented) could generate more `Heat`. High-value transactions.
*   **Decrease**: Laying low (not dealing for a few turns – passive decay, e.g., -1 `Heat` per turn if no illegal sales), specific actions via "Contacts" (e.g., "pay off a cop"), positive `World Events`.
*   **Effects**:
    *   **Graduated Impact**:
        *   *Low `Heat` (0-25%)*: Business as usual, perhaps some ambient police chatter in dialogues from customers or on the ambient phone UI. Minimal direct gameplay impact.
        *   *Medium `Heat` (26-50%)*: Increased chance of "police patrol" `World Events` (which might temporarily scare off certain customers or make transactions riskier). Some cautious customers (e.g., `REGULAR_JOE`) might comment on the increased police presence or be slightly less willing to deal. `INFORMANT`s might start selling info about specific police operations.
        *   *High `Heat` (51-75%)*: More frequent negative `World Events` (e.g., "Shakedown," where Rikk might lose some `Cash` or items if he encounters police; "Undercover Sting Operation Chance," where a customer interaction could be a setup). Some high-profile or cautious customers (e.g., `HIGH_ROLLER`, some `REGULAR_JOE`s) might refuse to meet or only deal in "safer" (less profitable) items. Prices offered by Rikk might need to be lower to convince wary buyers, or prices demanded by sellers to Rikk might be higher due to their perceived risk. `SNITCH`es become more likely to report any activity to the police.
        *   *Critical `Heat` (76-100%)*: Very high chance of "Police Raid" or "Bust" event leading to game over or severe penalties (e.g., confiscation of all inventory and most `Cash`, significant `Street Cred` loss). Most customers will refuse to deal. Ambient dialogues and phone messages reflect a city-wide crackdown. The "Customers" app might show fewer available interactions.
    *   Higher chance of negative random events (shakedowns, busts).
    *   Customers might be warier, prices less favorable.
    *   At max `Heat`, a "bust" or major raid becomes highly probable, potentially leading to game over or severe setbacks.
*   **Thematic Link**: `Heat` makes the "precarious world" tangible. It's the system pushing back against Rikk's hustle. Managing `Heat` is a core part of the strategic grind, forcing moral choices (e.g., dealing with a `SNITCH` might be profitable but increases `Heat` significantly). It represents the constant pressure and risk inherent in Rikk's lifestyle.

### Street Cred
#### Purpose & Goal
`Street Cred`'s purpose is to represent Rikk's reputation, influence, and trustworthiness within the local underground economy. Its goal is to serve as a progression metric that unlocks new gameplay opportunities and reflects Rikk's growing (or diminishing) status. It adds a layer of social strategy to the hustle, rewarding consistent, fair, or impactful dealings.

*   **Reputation Metric**: Represents Rikk's standing and trustworthiness in the underworld/local scene.
*   **Increase**: Successful deals (especially high-value or with important customers like `HIGH_ROLLER`s or by consistently providing good quality), completing "favors" (missions from Contacts), some customer dialogue choices (e.g., helping a customer out of a jam), positive `World Events`. Giving good deals or being generous.
*   **Decrease**: Bad deals (e.g., selling "Cut" drugs as "Pure" if a system for customer discovery is added), ripping people off (e.g., extreme lowball offers if a reputation hit mechanic is tied to it), being reported by a `SNITCH` (already implemented), being seen as unreliable.
*   **Effects**:
    *   **Unlock New Customer Archetypes**: Specific, more lucrative, or more dangerous customer archetypes might only appear once Rikk's `Street Cred` reaches a certain threshold (e.g., `HIGH_ROLLER` might require `Street Cred` > 10, a specialized `WEAPONS_DEALER` (future concept) might require `Street Cred` > 20). This provides a tangible reward for building reputation and expands gameplay variety.
    *   **Better Prices/Deals**: Higher `Street Cred` might make certain customer archetypes offer slightly better prices when buying from Rikk (e.g., their `priceToleranceFactor` is slightly adjusted in Rikk's favor for that interaction), or be more receptive to Rikk's haggling attempts (e.g., higher base success chance for haggling with them).
    *   **Access to Contacts**: The "Contacts" app could feature individuals (suppliers, info brokers, service providers) who only deal with Rikk if his `Street Cred` is sufficient. These contacts might offer better quality supplies, rare items, valuable information, or services like `Heat` reduction or legal aid.
    *   **Influence `World Events`/Opportunities**: High `Street Cred` might trigger unique positive `World Events` (e.g., "Big Shot from Out of Town Heard About You," leading to a lucrative, risky deal) or open up specific opportunities. Low `Street Cred` could trigger negative events (e.g., "Word is out Rikk's a Cheat," making customers wary).
    *   May influence certain narrative branches or opportunities, like being approached for a special "job" or having certain characters confide more in Rikk.
*   **Thematic Link**: `Street Cred` embodies the importance of reputation in informal economies. It shows that the hustle isn't just about quick cash; it's about building a name and trust (or fear). This can lead to better opportunities ("the grind paying off") but also potentially more significant risks as Rikk becomes more well-known. It reflects the complex social dynamics of the world Rikk inhabits.

### Player Skills
#### Purpose & Goal
Player skills aim to provide a light RPG layer, allowing players to specialize Rikk slightly and have a mechanical impact on common interactions. The purpose is to give players a sense of mastery and progression in specific aspects of the hustle. The goal is for these skills to offer tangible benefits that align with common challenges Rikk faces, like negotiation or avoiding trouble, adding another vector for player strategy and development.
*(Implemented skills are `negotiator`, `appraiser`, `lowProfile`.)*

*   **`negotiator`**:
    *   **Mechanical Impact**: This skill directly influences the success chance and outcome of haggling with customers who are not `negotiationResists: true`.
        *   *Increased Haggle Success Chance*: Each point in `negotiator` adds a percentage bonus to the base success chance of a haggle attempt (e.g., base 50% + 5% per skill point). This would be checked against a random roll in `GameManager.attemptHaggle()`.
        *   *Improved Haggle Outcome*: A successful haggle might yield a slightly better price. For example, if Rikk is selling, the final price might be `originalOffer + (skillBonusPercent * (RikksIdealPrice - originalOffer))`. If buying, `originalDemand - (skillBonusPercent * (originalDemand - RikksIdealPrice))`. The `skillBonusPercent` could scale with the `negotiator` skill (e.g., 10% base + 5% per skill point, capped).
        *   *Reduced Negative Impact of Failed Haggle*: A higher `negotiator` skill might reduce the chance of a customer getting angry or `Loyalty` dropping significantly after a failed haggle. For instance, a mood drop might be mitigated, or a `Loyalty` penalty halved.
*   **`appraiser`**:
    *   **Mechanical Impact**: Improves Rikk's ability to assess and influence item prices, and potentially identify item details.
        *   *More Accurate Value Assessment*: The displayed "estimated resale value" in the Stash app could become more accurate. For example, instead of a wide range, it might show a tighter range or even a more precise value as the skill increases. This helps in deciding what to keep or sell.
        *   *Improved Buy/Sell Prices (Subtle Influence)*:
            *   When Rikk sells to customers: The `CustomerManager._calculateItemValue` might factor in Rikk's `appraiser` skill to slightly nudge the `rikkBaseSellPrice` upwards before the customer's `priceToleranceFactor` is applied (e.g., `rikkBaseSellPrice * (1 + appraiserSkill * 0.01)`).
            *   When Rikk buys from customers: Similarly, the initial `customerDemandsPrice` might be subtly lowered if Rikk's `appraiser` skill is high, as he "knows" what it's worth and projects that confidence.
        *   *Identify Item Properties (Future)*: Higher `appraiser` skill could reveal hidden properties of items, such as if a "Stolen Good" is particularly "Hot" (more `Heat`), or if a "Drug" is of unusually high/low purity beyond its basic quality level, or even detect "Fake" items.
*   **`lowProfile`**:
    *   **Mechanical Impact**: Helps Rikk manage and reduce `Heat` generated from his activities.
        *   *Passive `Heat` Reduction*: Each turn, a small amount of `Heat` might decay naturally. Each point in `lowProfile` could increase this decay amount (e.g., base decay of -0.5 `Heat`, + an additional -0.5 `Heat` per point in `lowProfile`, ensuring it's noticeable over several turns). This decay would only apply if Rikk avoids high-`Heat` generating activities in a turn.
        *   *Reduced `Heat` Generation*: For transactions or actions that generate `Heat`, the amount of `Heat` generated could be reduced by a percentage based on the `lowProfile` skill (e.g., `generatedHeat * (1 - lowProfileSkill * 0.05)`), making risky deals slightly safer.
        *   *Increased Chance to Avoid Negative `Heat` Events*: May provide a small, scaling chance to avoid certain random `Heat`-related negative events like "Shakedown" or reduce their severity (e.g., lose less `Cash`).
*   **Progression Mechanism**: Rikk gains 1 Skill Point every time his `Street Cred` increases by 10 points. The player can then assign this point to `negotiator`, `appraiser`, or `lowProfile` via a new 'Skills' tab in the main phone UI (outside of apps). This makes `Street Cred` more directly rewarding with customizable character improvement.
*   **Thematic Link**: Skills represent Rikk getting better at his craft – the "art of the deal," knowing what's valuable, and learning how to stay off the radar. This progression shows Rikk adapting and specializing within the harsh realities of his world, reflecting the learning curve of survival in the streets. It offers a tangible way for the player to feel Rikk's growth beyond just accumulating `Cash`.

### Turn Progression
#### Purpose & Goal
The turn system provides the fundamental pacing and structure for the game. Its purpose is to break down the continuous "grind" into manageable segments where players make decisions and see their immediate impact. The goal is to create a clear cause-and-effect rhythm, allow for time-based `World Events` to unfold, and give a sense of time passing as Rikk navigates his precarious existence.

*   The game progresses in discrete turns, managed by `GameState.currentTurn`.
*   Each turn allows for one primary customer interaction or significant action.
*   Time-based effects (`World Events`, addiction craving) are tied to turn progression.
*   **Thematic Link**: The relentless tick of the turn counter emphasizes the ongoing nature of the hustle. Each new turn is a new day (or part of a day) with fresh risks and opportunities, reinforcing the theme of survival and the daily effort required to stay afloat. The turn-based nature also allows players to pause and consider their options, reflecting the calculated thinking needed for a successful hustle, rather than pure reflex.

#### Thematic Integration of Player State & Progression
The interconnected systems of `Cash`, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, and Skills are the pillars of Rikk's existence. `Cash` is the immediate fuel for the grind. `Heat` is the ever-present consequence of his actions, embodying the risk and precariousness of his world. `Street Cred` is the social capital, the hustle's long-term investment. Together, they create a dynamic state that the player must constantly monitor and manage, making choices that often involve trade-offs between these core stats, directly reflecting the game's themes of survival, risk, and moral ambiguity.

## Customer & Interaction System (Detailed)

#### Purpose & Goal
The customer and interaction system is the heart of "My Nigga Rikk." Its purpose is to bring the game world to life through its inhabitants, each with their own needs, personalities, and stories. The primary goal is to make each transaction more than just a numerical exchange, but a memorable interaction that can impact Rikk's stats, his understanding of the City, and his emotional state. This system is crucial for delivering the game's narrative, its moral ambiguity, and the feeling of a living, breathing (though dangerous) environment.

### Data Sources
*   **`data/customer_templates.js`**: The primary source for customer archetype definitions. Contains base stats, gameplay configurations (buy/sell preferences, price tolerance), and extensive dialogue trees with conditional logic.
*   **`data_items.js`**: Referenced by customer preferences for specific item IDs or types/subtypes.

### Customer Archetypes (data/customer_templates.js)
*   Each customer Rikk meets is an *instance* derived from an archetype in `customer_templates.js`.
*   **Key Properties Defined in Templates**:
    *   `key`: Unique identifier (e.g., "DESPERATE_FIEND").
    *   `baseName`: Used for generating instance names (e.g., "Jittery Jerry").
    *   `avatarUrl`: For potential UI display.
    *   `baseStats`: Initial `mood`, `loyalty` (now a 0-100 scale), `patience`, `relationship` (conceptual).
    *   `gameplayConfig`:
        *   `buyPreference`: Defines what items and qualities the customer wants to buy from Rikk. Can be complex with `or`, `and`, `exclude` logic, specific types, subtypes, or item IDs.
        *   `sellPreference`: Defines what items the customer might sell to Rikk, including item types, qualities, and `chance` of offering.
        *   `priceToleranceFactor`: How much more (or less) than base value they are willing to pay/accept.
            *   **Elaboration**: A `priceToleranceFactor` of 1.0 means they aim for the calculated base value. A value like 1.2 (e.g., for a `HIGH_ROLLER` buying) means they might offer up to 20% more. A value like 0.8 (e.g., for a `DESPERATE_FIEND` selling) means they might accept 20% less. This directly impacts Rikk's profit margins and the perceived "fairness" of a deal with different archetypes.
        *   `negotiationResists`: Boolean, if they resist haggling.
            *   **Elaboration**: If `true`, the "Haggle" option might be disabled, or attempting to haggle could immediately anger them or end the interaction, depending on design. This makes interactions with such customers more straightforward but less optimizable for Rikk.
        *   `heatImpact`, `credImpactSell`, `credImpactBuy`: How transactions with them affect Rikk's `Heat`/`Street Cred`.
            *   **Elaboration**: Selling a generic stolen good to a `REGULAR_JOE` might have `heatImpact: 1`, `credImpactSell: 1`. Selling a high-quality drug to a `HIGH_ROLLER` might have `heatImpact: 3`, `credImpactSell: 3`. Buying intel from an `INFORMANT` could have `credImpactBuy: 2` (good for Rikk) but `heatImpact: -1` (positive, as it helps avoid trouble). A `SNITCH` would have high `heatImpact` when Rikk sells to them. These values make customer choice a strategic decision beyond just price.
        *   `preferredDrugSubTypes`: If they are a drug user, what kinds they prefer (influences addiction).
        *   `sellsOnly`, `buysOnly`: Booleans to restrict interaction type.
    *   `dialogue`: A rich structure containing various dialogue contexts (greeting, negotiation, reactions to offers, etc.). Each context can have multiple dialogue blocks with:
        *   `conditions`: Based on customer stats (`mood`, `addictionStatus`, `Loyalty`), Rikk's stats (`Heat`, `Street Cred`), or game state (`World Events`).
        *   `lines`: Array of dialogue lines, one chosen randomly. Placeholders like `[ITEM_NAME]` and `[CUSTOMER_NAME]` are used.
        *   `payload`: Defines game effects triggered by that dialogue (e.g., mood change, event trigger).

### Dynamic Dialogue
*   The `CustomerManager._getDialogue(customerInstance, contextKey)` method is responsible for selecting appropriate dialogue:
    1.  It fetches the `dialogueNode` for the given `contextKey` (e.g., "greeting", "rikkDeclinesToBuy") from the customer's template.
    2.  It iterates through dialogue blocks within that node.
    3.  For each block, it checks `conditions` against the current `customerInstance` state (mood, `addictionStatus`, `Loyalty`) and `GameState` (e.g., active `World Events`, Rikk's `Heat`/`Street Cred`).
    4.  The first block whose conditions are all met has one of its `lines` randomly selected and processed for placeholders.
    5.  The associated `payload` is returned to be applied by the game logic.
*   **Further Influence from `GameState`**:
    *   Active `World Events` could directly influence dialogue. For example, if a "Police Crackdown" event is active, a `REGULAR_JOE` might have a conditional greeting: `{"conditions": [{"worldEventActive": "police_crackdown"}], "lines": ["Whoa Rikk, cops are swarming today, gotta be quick!"]}`.
    *   Rikk's `Heat`/`Street Cred`/`Cash` levels: Dialogue could change if Rikk is known to be very "hot" or very "broke." E.g., a customer might say, "Heard you're attracting `Heat`, Rikk. Make this fast," if `Heat` is high.

### Customer State & Pool Management
*   `CustomerManager._selectOrGenerateCustomerFromPool()`:
    *   Decides if a returning customer from `this.customersPool` appears (based on `CONFIG.RETURNING_CUSTOMER_CHANCE`).
    *   If returning, their `hasMetRikkBefore` flag is true. Their mood might reset to template base, `Cash` is re-randomized within returning customer ranges. `recentlySoldItems` is checked/pruned. `Loyalty` persists.
    *   If new, an archetype is chosen, an instance is created with a unique ID, name, initial stats from template (including base `Loyalty`, often starting low or neutral), randomized `Cash`, `hasMetRikkBefore: false`, and initialized `addictionStatus` and `recentlySoldItems`.
    *   The pool (`this.customersPool`) is managed (capped at `CONFIG.MAX_CUSTOMERS_IN_POOL`).
*   **Instance-Specific State**: Each customer instance (`customerInstance`) stores:
    *   Their current `mood`, `loyalty` (0-100 scale, influencing prices, dialogue, special offers), `patience`, `relationship` (conceptual, could be tied to `Loyalty` or be a separate metric for deeper narrative arcs).
    *   `cashOnHand`.
    *   `addictionStatus` object (`isAddicted`, `drugId`, `cravingLevel`).
    *   `hasMetRikkBefore` boolean.
    *   `currentItemName` (for placeholder replacement in dialogue).
    *   `recentlySoldItems` array (stores `itemInstanceId`s of items they sold to Rikk).

### Addiction System
*   **`CustomerManager.processPotentialAddiction(customerInstance, soldDrugItem)`**:
    *   Called when Rikk sells a drug to a customer.
    *   Checks `soldDrugItem.itemTypeObj.addictionChance`.
    *   If addiction roll succeeds:
        *   Sets `customerInstance.addictionStatus.isAddicted = true`.
        *   Sets `customerInstance.addictionStatus.drugId` to the `soldDrugItem.id`.
        *   Increments `cravingLevel` (capped), or sets to 1 if new addiction to this drug.
*   **Effects of Addiction**:
    *   **Dialogue**: Specific dialogue blocks can be conditioned on `addictionStatus.isAddicted` and `addictionStatus.drugId`. (e.g., `DESPERATE_FIEND`'s craving lines).
    *   **Buy Preference**: Addicted customers have a high chance (85% in current `generateInteraction`) to specifically demand their `drugId` if Rikk has it.
    *   **Price Tolerance**: `_calculateItemValue` increases the price an addicted customer is willing to pay for their drug of choice based on `cravingLevel`.
    *   **Further Behavioral Changes**:
        *   *Aggression/Desperation in Haggling*: Addicted customers, especially with high `cravingLevel`, might be more persistent or emotionally volatile during negotiations. Their `negotiationFail` dialogue could be more intense, and they might be less likely to accept a failed haggle without a significant `mood` or `Loyalty` drop.
        *   *Unique Dialogue Across Archetypes*: Beyond specific craving lines, an addicted `HIGH_ROLLER` might become more paranoid or erratic in their demands. An addicted `REGULAR_JOE` could express regret or anxiety in their `generalBanter` if their `mood` is also low. A `STIMULANT_USER` might have even more frenetic dialogue when craving.
        *   *Modified Sell Behavior*: An addicted customer with high `cravingLevel` and low `cashOnHand` could trigger a special sell condition. They might offer items not usually in their `sellPreference` (e.g., personal valuables like "Grandma's Locket" for a `DESPERATE_FIEND`) at a significantly reduced price, creating moral dilemmas for Rikk.
        *   *Impact on Other Stats*: High craving might temporarily override normal `patience` levels. It could also make them more susceptible to certain `World Events` (e.g., more likely to get into trouble if a "Police Crackdown" event is active).

## Inventory & Item System (Detailed)
#### Purpose & Goal
The Inventory & Item System serves as the backbone of the game's economy and the tangible representation of Rikk's hustle. Its purpose is to provide a diverse range of "goods" that facilitate player interaction, drive economic decisions, and reflect the realities of the game world. The primary goal is to make item management a key strategic element, where acquiring, evaluating, and selling items at the right time to the right customer is crucial for success, directly tying into the core theme of "the grind" and resourcefulness.

### Item Definitions (data_items.js)
*   `ITEM_TYPES`: An array of objects, each defining a sellable item.
    *   `id`: Unique string identifier (e.g., "aspirin", "rolex_knockoff", "info_rival_dealer").
    *   `name`: Display name.
    *   `description`: Flavor text.
    *   `type`: Broad category (e.g., "DRUG", "STOLEN_GOOD", "INFORMATION", "ODDITY").
    *   `subType` (optional): Specific category (e.g., "OPIATE", "ELECTRONICS", "WEAPON_PART"). Used for preferences.
    *   `baseValue`: Average base monetary value.
    *   `range`: +/- range for randomizing actual value when generated.
        *   **Elaboration**: When an item is generated for a customer to sell (in `_generateRandomItem`), its initial `purchasePrice` (what the customer will ask Rikk for) is influenced by `baseValue` and `range`. The current implementation uses `Math.round(selectedType.baseValue * qualityPriceModifier * (0.8 + Math.random() * 0.4))`. This random multiplier effectively serves the purpose of a `range`, introducing variability so not every "Gold Watch" has the exact same underlying value. This makes Rikk's appraisal skills and market knowledge more important.
    *   `heat`: Base `Heat` generated when Rikk sells this item. This is a planned property that would allow certain items (e.g., "Illegal Firearm") to inherently carry more risk than others (e.g., "Stolen Sneakers").
    *   `addictionChance` (for DRUG type): Probability of causing/progressing addiction.
    *   `effects`: An array of structured effect objects that can be applied upon transaction or use.
        *   **Elaboration**: For example, `{ "type": "moodChange", "target": "customer", "value": "happy", "chance": 0.5 }` or `{ "type": "statChange", "target": "player", "stat": "heat", "amount": -2 }` if Rikk uses a "Police Scanner Info" item.
*   `ITEM_QUALITY_LEVELS`: Object mapping item `type` to an array of quality names (e.g., "DRUG": ["Cut", "Standard", "Pure"]).
    *   **Elaboration on Assignment**: In `_generateRandomItem`, if a `sellPreference` specifies a particular `quality` (index), that is used. If a `maxQuality` is specified, a random quality up to that max is chosen. Otherwise, quality is chosen randomly from the available levels for that item's `type`. This ensures variety but also allows templates to define if a customer typically sells low or high-quality goods. "Weird items" currently default to quality index 0 ("Standard" or equivalent).
*   `ITEM_QUALITY_MODIFIERS`: Object mapping item `type` to an array of price multipliers corresponding to quality levels.

### Item Effects & Mechanics
*   **Pricing**:
    *   `CustomerManager._generateRandomItem()`: When a customer generates an item to sell to Rikk, its `purchasePrice` (what the customer *asks* for) is `Math.max(CONFIG.MIN_ITEM_PRICE, Math.round(selectedType.baseValue * qualityPriceModifier * (0.8 + Math.random() * 0.4)))`. Its `estimatedResaleValue` (what Rikk might sell it for later) is currently set to this `purchasePrice`.
    *   `CustomerManager._calculateItemValue()`: Determines the actual transaction price when Rikk is selling to a customer, or what Rikk considers offering when buying. It factors in the item's inherent value, quality, player skills (`appraiser`), `World Events` (price modifiers), customer `priceToleranceFactor`, and `addictionStatus.cravingLevel`.
*   **Instance ID**:
    *   Items sold by customers to Rikk get a unique `itemInstanceId` assigned in `CustomerManager.generateInteraction`.
    *   This allows tracking of specific item instances, primarily to prevent customers from buying back items they just sold.
*   **Addiction**: As detailed in the Customer System, drugs have an `addictionChance`.
*   **`Heat`/`Street Cred`**: Transactions involving items contribute to `Heat`/`Street Cred` changes, often determined by the customer's archetype config and the nature of the item (e.g., a future item-specific `heat` property).
*   **Descriptive Drug Effects (e.g., `paranoia_high_dose`, `jitters`)**:
    *   **Mechanical Translation**: These are currently primarily flavor text in item descriptions. To give them mechanical weight:
        *   `paranoia_high_dose`: An item with this descriptor in `data_items.js` could have an associated effect object like: `{"effectId": "induce_paranoia", "onUse": [{"type": "moodChange", "target": "customer", "value": "paranoid", "duration": 3, "chance": 0.7}]}`. When Rikk sells it, if the `chance` roll succeeds, the customer's `mood` changes to "paranoid" for 3 turns.
        *   `jitters`: Could be linked to an effect like `{"effectId": "cause_jitters", "onTransaction": [{"type": "statChange", "target": "player", "stat": "heat", "amount": 1}]}`. The `publicIncident` event for `DESPERATE_FIEND` is a good specific example; this could be generalized.
        *   `euphoria_mild`: Could have an effect like `{"effectId": "mild_euphoria", "onUse": [{"type": "moodChange", "target": "customer", "value": "happy", "duration": 1}, {"type": "tempModifier", "target": "customer", "stat": "priceToleranceFactor", "amount": 1.1, "duration": 1}]}`. This would make them happier and slightly more generous for their next immediate interaction if they "use" it (which is an implied effect post-purchase).
    *   This requires adding an `effects` array to item definitions in `data_items.js` and logic in `GameManager` or `CustomerManager` to parse and apply these effects when an item is sold or (if Rikk could use items) used.

### Inventory Management (GameState.inventory)
*   `GameState.inventory`: An array storing item objects that Rikk possesses.
*   **Adding Items**: When Rikk buys from a customer or acquires items through other means (future).
*   **Removing Items**: When Rikk sells to a customer.
*   **Capacity**: `CONFIG.INVENTORY_FULL_THRESHOLD` limits how many items Rikk can hold. If full, he cannot buy more. This creates strategic choices about which items to keep or drop if inventory is full and a good opportunity arises.
*   **Recently Sold Tracking**: Each `customerInstance` has a `recentlySoldItems` array. When Rikk buys an item from a customer, that item's `itemInstanceId` is added. This prevents customers from immediately trying to buy back the same item instance.

#### Thematic Integration of Inventory & Item System
The items themselves – their types, descriptions, and qualities – are deeply thematic, painting a picture of the street economy. The "Stolen Goods" imply a network of theft, "Drugs" point to addiction, "Information" to the value of secrets, and "Oddities" to the strange encounters in the City. Managing inventory under a capacity limit reinforces the precariousness of Rikk's operation – he can't hoard everything. The pricing variations and quality levels make the "hustle" of finding good deals and quality suppliers (or customers) a core gameplay challenge. The potential for items to have direct mechanical effects (addiction, `Heat`, mood changes) further ties them into the game's simulation of a risky, reactive world.

## World Event System (Detailed)
#### Purpose & Goal
The `World Event` System is designed to make the game world feel dynamic, unpredictable, and reactive to both the passage of time and the player's actions. Its purpose is to introduce narrative flavor, new challenges, and temporary opportunities that force the player to adapt their strategies. The primary goal is to ensure that no two playthroughs feel exactly the same and to constantly reinforce the theme of a precarious, ever-changing environment where Rikk must stay vigilant.

### Event Definitions
*   Stored in `data/world_events.js`. Each event is an object:
    *   `id`: Unique identifier.
    *   `name`: Display name/headline.
    *   `description`: Flavor text explaining the event.
    *   `trigger`: Conditions for the event to occur (e.g., `turnNumber`, `heatLevel`, `credLevel`, `randomChance`, completion of another event or specific player action).
        *   **Elaboration on Triggers**:
            *   `turnNumber`: `{"type": "turn", "min": 50, "max": 100}` (triggers between turn 50-100).
            *   `heatLevel`: `{"type": "heat", "gte": 75}` (triggers if `Heat` is 75 or more).
            *   `credLevel`: `{"type": "cred", "lte": -5}` (triggers if `Street Cred` is -5 or less).
            *   `randomChance`: `{"type": "random", "chance": 0.05}` (5% chance each turn, perhaps after a certain initial turn number).
            *   `specificPlayerAction`: Could be conceptual, e.g., after Rikk successfully sells 10 "Pure" quality drugs, an event "High_Quality_Noticed" could trigger. This would require more complex event listener logic in `GameManager`.
            *   `previousEventCompleted`: `{"type": "eventCompleted", "eventId": "drug_bust_downtown"}` (triggers after "drug_bust_downtown" finishes).
    *   `duration`: How many turns the event lasts (can be immediate, fixed, or until a condition is met).
    *   `effects`: An array of game state modifications, e.g.:
        *   `type: "price_change", itemType: "DRUG", subType: "OPIATE", modifier: 1.5` (Opiates 50% more expensive).
        *   `type: "heat_change", amount: 10`.
        *   `type: "item_availability", itemPool: ["rare_item_1"], available: true/false`.
        *   `type: "spawn_customer_type", archetypeKey: "HIGH_ROLLER", chance: 0.5`.
            *   **Elaboration**: This could temporarily increase the probability of the `HIGH_ROLLER` archetype appearing via `CustomerManager._selectOrGenerateCustomerFromPool()`, or even force a specific encounter if `chance: 1.0`.
        *   `type: "dialogue_override", customerArchetype: "REGULAR_JOE", context: "greeting", newText: "Cops are everywhere, man!"`.
            *   **Elaboration**: This is a more complex effect. A simpler way is for dialogue conditions in `customer_templates.js` to directly check for active `World Events` (e.g., `{"condition": "worldEventActive", "eventId": "police_crackdown"}`). However, a direct override could inject temporary, highly specific lines.
        *   `type: "stat_modifier", target: "player", stat: "luck", amount: 1, duration: 5`.
    *   `itemScarcity`: Boolean, if true, reduces chance of customers having items to sell.
    *   `drugDemandModifier`: Multiplier for likelihood of customers wanting drugs.
    *   `specificItemDemand`: Array of item IDs that customers might specifically ask for.
    *   `customerScareChance`: Chance a customer interaction is aborted due to perceived danger.
    *   `allPriceModifier`: Global price multiplier.
    *   `drugPriceModifier`: Specific price multiplier for drugs.

### Activation & Management
*   `WorldEventManager.updateEvents(gameState)`: Called each turn.
    1.  **Decrement Durations**: Decreases duration for active events, removes expired ones.
    2.  **Check Triggers**: Iterates through inactive events in `data/world_events.js`. If an event's `trigger` conditions are met by the current `GameState`, it becomes active.
    3.  **Activation Limit**: Potentially limit the number of concurrent active events (e.g., max 2-3) to avoid overwhelming the player or creating conflicting effects.
    4.  **Mutual Exclusivity**: Define rules if some events cannot occur simultaneously (e.g., a "Police Strike" event and a "Police Crackdown" event shouldn't be active together). This could be managed with event tags or an exclusion list in event definitions.
    5.  **Chaining**: Some events might trigger successor events upon completion (e.g., "Investigation_Starts" could lead to "Indictment_Looms" if `Heat` isn't reduced).
*   `activeWorldEvents`: An array in `GameState` storing instances of currently active events, including their remaining duration.

### Effect Application
*   `WorldEventManager.applyActiveEventEffects(gameState)` or `WorldEventManager.getCombinedWorldEffects(gameState.activeWorldEvents)`:
    *   Iterates through `gameState.activeWorldEvents`.
    *   Aggregates all effects from active events into a `combinedWorldEffects` object. For example, multiple price modifiers for the same item type are compounded or averaged. Stat modifiers are summed.
    *   This `combinedWorldEffects` object is then used by other systems (`CustomerManager`, `UIManager`) to modify behavior, prices, dialogue, etc.
    *   Example: `CustomerManager._calculateItemValue` uses `combinedWorldEffects.drugPriceModifier` or `combinedWorldEffects.allPriceModifier`. `CustomerManager._generateRandomItem` uses `itemScarcity`.
    *   Notifications about new or impactful events are shown to the player via the phone UI.

#### Thematic Integration of `World Event` System
`World Events` are key to making the game's City feel alive, reactive, and fundamentally precarious. They introduce elements beyond Rikk's direct control, simulating the external shocks and systemic changes that impact anyone in the hustle. Events like police crackdowns directly increase risk, while item shortages or new drug demands reflect the volatile market Rikk operates in. This system reinforces the theme that Rikk is not in a vacuum; he's a small player in a larger, often chaotic, urban ecosystem. Successfully navigating these events is a core part of the "grind" and requires adaptability.

## Game State & Data Persistence (Detailed)
#### Purpose & Goal
The Game State and Data Persistence system is the technical foundation that allows for a continuous and evolving player experience. Its purpose is to reliably store all information that defines the current state of Rikk's world and his progression within it. The primary goal is to ensure that players can stop and resume their game seamlessly, preserving their efforts, choices, and the unique state of their playthrough, which is essential for a game that aims to tell an emergent story over many turns.

### Central State
*   `GameState` (managed in `main.js` or a dedicated `GameStateManager.js`): A single large object holding all current game information.
    *   **Importance of Single Source of Truth**: Having a centralized `GameState` object is crucial for game stability and consistency. It prevents data conflicts that might arise if different parts of the game were trying to manage their own independent copies of state variables. All game logic reads from and writes to this central object, ensuring that every manager and UI component is working with the same, up-to-date information. This simplifies debugging and makes it easier to implement save/load functionality reliably.
*   This includes:
    *   `currentTurn`: Integer.
    *   `cash`: Player's money.
    *   `heat`: Current police attention level.
    *   `streetCred`: Player's reputation.
    *   `inventory`: Array of item objects Rikk owns. Each item object includes `id`, `name`, `quality`, `qualityIndex`, `itemTypeObj` (a copy of its base definition from `data_items.js`), `purchasePrice` (what Rikk paid or its generated value if from customer), and `itemInstanceId` (if applicable).
    *   `playerSkills`: Object with skill names and their levels (e.g., `{ negotiator: 1, appraiser: 0, lowProfile: 0 }`).
    *   `activeWorldEvents`: Array of active `World Event` objects, including their remaining duration and effects.
    *   `customerPool`: Array of unique customer instances managed by `CustomerManager.customersPool` (this might be saved via `CustomerManager.getSaveState()`).
    *   `nextCustomerId`: Counter from `CustomerManager`.
    *   Phone-related state if needed (e.g., unlocked apps, current wallpaper - though `UIManager` might handle its own visual state).

### Initialization
*   `main.js` initializes the game:
    1.  Loads all static data: `customer_templates.js`, `data_items.js`, `phone_apps.js`, `world_events.js`, `phone_ambient_ui.js`.
    2.  Instantiates managers: `CustomerManager`, `WorldEventManager`, `UIManager`.
    3.  Initializes `GameState` with starting values (`Cash`, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, turn 1, empty inventory, base skills).
    4.  `UIManager` renders the initial phone UI.

### Saving & Loading
*   **Saving**:
    *   Can occur automatically at the end of each turn or via a manual "Save Game" option in the Settings app.
    *   The `GameStateManager` (or `main.js`) would gather all relevant state information:
        *   Core `GameState` properties (turn, `Cash`, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, inventory, skills, `activeWorldEvents`).
        *   `CustomerManager.getSaveState()` to get `customersPool` and `nextCustomerId`.
    *   This entire state object is serialized to JSON and saved to `localStorage` (or other persistence layer).
*   **Loading**:
    *   Typically from a "Load Game" option.
    *   Reads the JSON string from `localStorage`.
    *   Deserializes it back into an object.
    *   Restores `GameState` with the loaded data.
    *   Calls `CustomerManager.loadSaveState(loadedCustomerManagerState)` to repopulate the customer pool and ID counter.
    *   `WorldEventManager` might need to re-initialize its state based on loaded `activeWorldEvents` (e.g., ensuring effects are correctly re-applied or timers are accurate).
    *   `UIManager` refreshes the display based on the loaded state.
*   **Data Integrity & Versioning (Future Concern)**:
    *   **Edge Cases/Challenges**:
        *   *Saving Mid-Interaction*: Ideally, saving should occur at clean points (e.g., end of turn). Saving in the middle of a customer dialogue or a multi-step action could lead to inconsistent states upon loading. If allowed, the exact point of interaction would need to be saved and restored, which is complex.
        *   *Handling Data Structure Changes*: If game updates change the structure of `GameState` or objects within it (e.g., adding a new property to items or customers), loading an older save file could cause errors or missing data. A simple approach is to invalidate old saves. A more robust approach involves:
            *   *Versioning*: Include a version number in the save file.
            *   *Migration Logic*: Write specific functions to "upgrade" an older save version to the current structure, filling in missing fields with defaults or transforming data as needed. This is complex but essential for long-term support if the game is updated post-release.
            *   *Graceful Failure*: If an old save is truly incompatible and cannot be migrated, the game should detect this and inform the player gracefully, rather than crashing.
*   **Thematic Link**: The ability to save and load, while a standard game feature, can thematically be seen as Rikk "sleeping on it" or the player reflecting on their choices. The persistence of `customerPool` and their states (like addiction) across sessions makes the world feel more continuous and Rikk's relationships (or exploitations) more meaningful, as they have lasting impact. The risk of a corrupted save (though a technical issue) could even be a meta-commentary on the precariousness of data in the digital age, though that's a stretch!

#### Thematic Integration of Game State & Data Persistence
While largely a technical necessity, the persistence of game state reinforces the long-term consequences of Rikk's hustle. Past deals, customer relationships (even if simply `hasMetRikkBefore`), and Rikk's accumulated `Heat` and `Street Cred` carry over, making the "grind" a continuous narrative rather than a series of disconnected days. The reliability of this system is key to player investment in Rikk's journey through his precarious world.

## UI Management & Style System (Detailed)
#### Purpose & Goal
The UI Management and Style System is responsible for presenting the game world and interactions to the player through the singular lens of Rikk's phone. Its purpose is to create a diegetic, immersive, and intuitive interface that thematically aligns with the "street hustle" performed via a mobile device. The primary goal is to ensure all game information is clearly communicated and all player actions are easily executable, while maintaining a consistent aesthetic that reflects the game's gritty, urban setting and Rikk's personality.

### UIManager.js Functionality
*   **DOM Manipulation**: Responsible for all direct interactions with the HTML document (creating elements, setting text, adding/removing classes, handling event listeners for UI elements).
*   **Rendering Views**:
    *   `renderPhoneShell()`: Draws the main phone outline, status bar (time, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, `Cash`), and navigation bar (app icons).
    *   `renderApp(appId)`: Clears the current app display area and calls a specific rendering function for the given `appId` (e.g., `_renderCustomersApp()`, `_renderStashApp()`).
    *   `_renderCustomersApp(interaction)`: Displays customer avatar (if any), dialogue lines, and interaction choices based on the object from `CustomerManager.generateInteraction()`.
    *   `_renderStashApp(inventory)`: Displays the player's inventory items.
    *   `displayNotification(message, type)`: Shows modal or toast-like notifications (e.g., for `World Events`, errors).
    *   `updateStatusDisplays(gameState)`: Updates the `Cash`, `Heat`, `Street Cred`, `Turn #` displays.
*   **Event Listeners**:
    *   Sets up click listeners for app icons on the nav bar.
    *   Sets up click listeners for dialogue choices during customer interactions.
    *   These listeners typically call handler functions in `main.js` or the relevant manager.
*   **State Management (UI-specific)**: May hold some internal state about the currently active app or view.
*   **Style Application**: Adds/removes CSS classes to elements to change their appearance based on game state or UI state (e.g., disabling buttons, highlighting active app).

### Style Customization
*   **CSS Variables**: The primary method for theming. `style_manager.js` (or similar) would define functions to change CSS custom property values on the `:root` or specific elements.
    *   `--phone-bg-color`, `--text-color`, `--accent-color`, `--button-bg-color`, `--header-font`, etc.
*   **`style_manager.js` (Conceptual)**:
    *   `applyStyle(styleObject)`: Takes an object with CSS variable key-value pairs and applies them.
        ```javascript
        // Example styleObject for a "Dark Mode"
        // const darkMode = {
        //     "--phone-bg-color": "#1e1e1e",
        //     "--text-color": "#ffffff",
        //     "--accent-color": "#bb86fc"
        // };
        // styleManager.applyStyle(darkMode);
        ```
    *   Could have pre-defined themes (objects like `darkMode` above) that can be selected by the player in Settings.
    *   Could also allow individual property changes if granular customization is desired (more complex).
*   **CSS File (`style.css`)**:
    *   Defines base styles for all UI elements (phone, apps, buttons, text).
    *   Uses CSS variables extensively (e.g., `background-color: var(--phone-bg-color);`).
    *   May contain different class definitions for different visual states (e.g., `.button-disabled`, `.notification-error`).
*   **Font Management**: Could allow changing `--header-font`, `--body-font` via `style_manager.js` by setting them to different font-family names (assuming those fonts are imported/available).
*   **Dynamic Class Binding**: `UIManager` changes element classes based on state. E.g., if a choice is disabled, `buttonElement.classList.add('disabled-choice');`. `style.css` defines what `.disabled-choice` looks like.
*   **Thematic Link of UI Customization**: Allowing Rikk (the player) to customize his phone's theme (wallpaper, color scheme) can be a small but significant form of player expression and immersion. In a world where Rikk has little control, personalizing his primary tool – his phone – can be a minor act of agency and individuality. It can reflect his "style" or current `mood`, subtly connecting the player to Rikk's persona. For instance, a player feeling successful might opt for a flashier theme, while one feeling the `Heat` might choose something more subdued.

#### Thematic Integration of UI & Style
The UI *is* the game world for the player. By confining interactions to a phone interface, the game immediately establishes a modern, relatable, yet potentially constrained environment. The style – from fonts to color schemes – should evoke the urban, gritty, and somewhat underground nature of Rikk's hustle. Notifications and ambient messages mimic real-world phone usage, enhancing immersion and the sense of a persistent, ongoing world that Rikk needs to stay plugged into. The UI's efficiency and directness in presenting choices and information reflect the fast-paced, decision-driven nature of "the life."

## Enhancing Depth: Connecting Mechanics to Theme & Message

This is where the game stops being just a series of button presses and becomes a story, a statement. It's about making sure every piece of the puzzle – every deal, every risk, every character – carries the weight of the world Rikk lives in, the "reality of how it is."

### Integrating "The Message" into Every Action
The game's core message isn't in a cutscene; it's in the click of a button, the choice Rikk makes when his back's against the wall.
*   **The Transaction Tango**: When Rikk haggles with a `DESPERATE_FIEND`, the numbers on the screen should feel secondary to the human drama. Is he pushing someone to their breaking point for a few extra bucks? The UI might reflect this – the fiend's avatar looking more distressed, their dialogue becoming more erratic. The "win" of a good price might leave a bitter taste. This isn't just about resource exchange; it's about power dynamics and desperation.
*   **Inventory as a Moral Ledger**: Rikk's stash isn't just items; it's a reflection of his choices. A stash full of hard drugs and cheap stolen goods tells one story. A stash with more information, perhaps some "cleaner" items, tells another. The act of choosing what to buy from a supplier, or what to accept from a customer, becomes a moral decision point. If Rikk is forced to sell something he'd rather not because he's broke, that reinforces the theme of systemic pressure.
*   **`Heat` & `Street Cred` as Social Barometers**: These aren't just scores. When `Heat` is high, the ambient messages on Rikk's phone should reflect paranoia – "Unknown number: Heard they lookin 4 u." Customer dialogues should become more cautious. This makes managing `Heat` an act of navigating a hostile environment, not just gaming a system. High `Street Cred` might unlock dialogues where customers confide in Rikk, offering glimpses into their lives beyond the transaction, making the world feel more interconnected and Rikk's reputation more meaningful.
*   **The Weight of Addiction**: When Rikk sells to an addicted customer, especially one whose `cravingLevel` is high, the game should make this felt. Perhaps the transaction yields more `Cash`, but also a higher chance of a negative follow-up event (the customer ODs, gets busted, causes trouble for Rikk). This turns a simple sale into a gamble with human consequences, directly reflecting "the reality of how it is" for those caught in the cycle.

### Expanding on Mentioned Features (for thematic/narrative elements)
The game's design already has seeds for powerful thematic expression; they just need to be nurtured.
*   **The "Close Friend's Story" – Echoes in the System**: This real-world inspiration is the soul of the game's potential message. It shouldn't be a direct plot point, but its essence – the struggle, the systemic failures, the human cost of addiction and poverty – must be woven into the game's DNA.
    *   **Customer Archetypes as Reflections**: The `DESPERATE_FIEND` is the most obvious, but even a `REGULAR_JOE` could, through a series of bad turns or choices (perhaps influenced by Rikk's sales), start down a path of addiction. Their dialogue, their changing needs, their physical description (if avatars were dynamic) could subtly mirror aspects of the friend's journey, creating a poignant, player-driven narrative arc.
    *   **`World Events` as Systemic Commentary**: Events like "Overdose Spike in Ward C" or "Halfway House Loses Funding" directly reflect the societal context that contributes to such tragedies. These events make the friend's story not an isolated incident, but part of a larger, systemic problem the game world acknowledges.
    *   **Moments of Choice & Helplessness**: Rikk might encounter a customer clearly echoing the friend's situation. The game could offer Rikk choices: exploit them, ignore them, or perhaps a very limited, costly, and not always successful way to "help" (e.g., pointing them to a barely-functional clinic via a Contact, which might cost Rikk resources and have a low chance of success for the NPC). This highlights Rikk's (and the player's) limited agency in the face of overwhelming systemic issues, which can be a powerful narrative statement.
*   **Evolving Customer Relationships – Beyond the Deal**:
    *   If `Loyalty` or `relationship` stats are deepened, they can unlock narrative content. A loyal customer might share a personal story, ask Rikk for a non-transactional favor (e.g., "Can you keep an eye out for my kid brother, he's been running with a bad crowd?"), or offer a piece of crucial information not for sale, but out of trust. This turns customers from mere resources into flawed human beings Rikk has a history with.
*   **Item Provenance & Impact**:
    *   The `itemInstanceId` system can be expanded. If Rikk buys a "Stolen Medkit" from one customer and later sells it to another who desperately needs it for a sick child, the game can acknowledge this chain of events, perhaps through a unique dialogue or a small, unexpected `Street Cred` gain. This adds a sense of consequence and interconnectedness to the items themselves.
    *   Drugs having more tangible short-term effects on customer dialogue or behavior post-sale (e.g., a customer sold a "Paranoia Special" might send Rikk a series of paranoid texts a few turns later) would make the act of selling these items more narratively impactful.

### Making the Unseen Felt
The City's systemic pressures and hidden actors need to cast longer shadows over Rikk's hustle.
*   **Systemic Pressure through Scarcity and Demand**: `World Events` like "Recession Hits Hard" shouldn't just be a text notification; they should mean customers have less `Cash`, demand cheaper (often lower quality or more dangerous) goods, and are more desperate. Rikk feels the economic pinch directly. "Police Budget Increased" could mean new, tougher patrol patterns or even new "undercover" customer types appearing.
*   **Rivals as Active (But Unseen) Agents**:
    *   Instead of just abstract price changes, Rikk might get a text from a Contact: "Yo, Z-Block Kings are moving heavy on your turf, watch your back." This could trigger a temporary increase in `customerScareChance` or certain customers being unavailable.
    *   If Rikk's `Heat` gets too high in a specific (even abstracted) area, a rival might "claim" that spot, meaning certain customers associated with that area stop appearing for a while, with dialogue indicating they "found a new supplier."
*   **The Police as More Than "`Heat`"**:
    *   Ambient sounds of sirens that get closer if Rikk stays in one interaction too long or makes several deals in a short number of turns (implied through text if no actual audio).
    *   Customers nervously ending interactions early: "Gotta bounce, Rikk, that patrol car just passed again."
    *   The "Sting Operation" `World Event` could introduce a customer who seems normal but whose dialogue choices, if Rikk isn't careful, lead to an immediate bust.

### Reinforcing Moral Ambiguity and Systemic Issues
The game should constantly challenge the player with choices that have no easy answers, reflecting the complex realities of survival.
*   **The "Lesser of Two Evils" Choice**: Rikk might need a rare component to fulfill a lucrative request from a `HIGH_ROLLER`. His only source is an `INFORMANT` who demands Rikk sell a dangerous item to a vulnerable `DESPERATE_FIEND` in exchange for the information. This forces a choice with clear moral trade-offs and potential long-term consequences for multiple parties.
*   **Consequence Loops**:
    *   Selling a lot of hard drugs might increase `Street Cred` with some archetypes (`HIGH_ROLLER`s, some `DESPERATE_FIEND`s) but also increases overall city despair, potentially leading to more police raids or negative `World Events` that affect everyone, including Rikk.
    *   Helping one customer might anger another who is their rival or who sees Rikk as "soft."
*   **Environmental Storytelling in Dialogue**:
    *   Customers don't just ask for items; they reveal *why*. "Lost my job, man, need something to take the edge off." "My kid's sick, Rikk, you got anything cheap that looks valuable? Gotta pawn it." These snippets build a world of systemic failure without overt exposition.
    *   A `SNITCH` might justify their actions: "Look, I got kids to feed too. Everyone's gotta survive, right?" This complicates their role beyond simple antagonism.
*   **Player's Complicity and Justification**: The game should, through its mechanics, allow Rikk to become successful by making morally questionable choices. If Rikk becomes wealthy by exploiting addiction, the game doesn't wag a finger. Instead, perhaps Rikk's internal monologue (rare text pop-ups) becomes more cynical or detached, or the "endings" reflect the hollowness of success built on others' misery. The "message" is more potent if the player arrives at these conclusions through their own actions and reflections rather than being explicitly told.

By weaving these narrative and thematic considerations deeply into the core mechanics and player interactions, "My Nigga Rikk" can become a more compelling, immersive, and thought-provoking experience that truly "paints a full picture" of the world it aims to represent.
